# Spirituality: Religion in Disguise

For centuries or even millennia, religion acted as a central institution for human society. Religions institutionalized the concept of God and established a set of rules that were believed to bring people closer to God (or the divine). However, with advancements in science, education, storage of verified knowledge, and information systems, **the degree of religious faith among individuals and society has significantly declined**. This suggests that religion and the idea of God were historically crucial to sustain societal structure, uniformity, and sustainability – like the constitution and laws in the contemporary world. Nevertheless, with the passage of time, **they have simply begun to lose their grip over people's mind and the society.**

The term *"spirituality"* has gained a high degree of popularity across the world, not just in South Asia. Still, *what is the meaning of spirituality, at first?* The answer is not singular, as everyone holds a subjective definition of it. This vagueness makes the question more worth asking.

Many key philosophers and psychologists suggest different definitions of spirituality. However, here, I would emphasize how people generally understand it. When I consulted the Cambridge Dictionary, it showed, spirituality is "the quality that involves deep feelings and beliefs of a religious nature, rather than the physical parts of life." Like many words, the meaning of spirituality might also change eventually with the flow of time.

This means that spirituality fundamentally focuses on the non-materialistic or inner aspects of life. It emphasizes on an individual's bond with the universe or their own "**inner self**". It encourages individuals to explore where they personally find meaning – unlike traditional religions which impose strict obligations, rites, and rituals to reach God.

Before we draw any conclusions or make assumptions about spirituality, we must take some social and natural facts into accounts – which is very necessary here.

**First, the credibility and relevance of traditional religion have largely collapsed in the past century** (or are gradually collapsing) due to accessibility to education, information, and knowledge. The decline in the percentage of religious people in censuses in developed and some developing nations is arguably the most concrete evidence of it. The population of religiously unaffiliated people has risen from 1.6 billion people (23%) in 2010, to 1.9 billion (24%) in 2020.

[*Pew Research Center: Religiously Unaffiliated Population Change*](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/06/09/religiously-unaffiliated-population-change/)

**Second, many people feel lonely, making them seek validation, reassurance, and a sense of belonging.** Both religion and spirituality provide the very psychological supplements to meet these needs.

**Third, people naturally experience moments of aha, epiphanies, and awe – which are indeed genuine and deeply meaningful.** Some genuine human feelings of awe and wonder were punished or suppressed by rigid religious beliefs. Also, they were often redirected to God (or to the divine). Following the fall of religious credibility, **there are many people who feel lost in this transition period.** Now, they have no place to worship, pray, or confess with complete faith which leaves them isolated. Religion remained as the trusted institution to anchor their morality, ethics, behaviour, and emotions.

I personally find the modern idea of spirituality to be quite ***two-faced***. **The basic building blocks of both religion and spirituality are almost identical**. As we know, spirituality claims to oppose dogmatic communal obligations. But it has its own hierarchy of spiritual teachers (Gurus) and **relies heavily on unfalsifiable claims.** That is ironic! **Spirituality has effectively turned into a profit-driven industry selling people back their own egos in sacred makeup.**

Amidst all of this, my primary concern is that **spirituality may simply be the rebirth of religion.** Historically, religions served as vital drivers to the economy, redistributing wealth through institutionalized obligations even though that was essentially hypocritical. The modern spirituality industry operates on the exact **same framework**. It has **commercialized a psychologically fundamental human need: the desire to belong.** *People used to donate money or any other form of wealth to temples and churches; now, they pay for "spiritual" workshops.* Even though spirituality projects an identity of open-accessibility, **numerous commercial ideas hide beneath it.** For instance, preposterous **retreat prices** advertised as *"spiritual investments"* or even certifications based on rankings. Retreat pricing might be justifiable, but how can you ever justify the certifications-based rankings?

If a spirit or any kind of spiritual essence resides within every person, why is financial transaction required to get a close access to it?

*In this sense, spirituality replicates platforms like Spotify, Claude, Canva, etc. The common principle is offering a basic version to all the users but reserve the "authentic" experience for premium paying customers.*

When we talk about spirituality, the word *"**spirit**"* also follows. Regarding this term, [**Jung**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Jung) defined it as an autonomous, non-material psychological reality experienced as an active, dynamic force. He argued that it brings meaning, inspiration, and higher consciousness to the human mind. He perceived the spirit as a subjective psychological core rather than any kind of metaphysical entity. He also relates it to the psyche, the deep mental structures driving human behaviour. ([Source: Gitz-Johansen, *Journal of Analytical Psychology*, 2020](https://www.jungiananalysts.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Gitz-Johansen-T.-JAP-2020-65-4-653-671-1.pdf))

On the contrary, the so-called "spiritual leaders" push an absurdly **unfalsifiable and metaphysical** definition of the spirit. Oftentimes, they rob some natural and scientifically explainable emotional states like awe, aha, and epiphanies. They repackage simple human experiences into out-of-reality stories and fictions merely for commercial gain and socio-political influence.

*Spirituality has acknowledged that external deities and authoritarian religions are losing credibility. Which is why, it has shifted its focus from the external to the internal also known as "the inner self." It has basically relocated the office of faith-based influence from an external God to an "internalized spirit" or "cosmic self".*

By convincing followers that the entire universe resides within them, **spiritual leaders present standard human emotions as evidence of cosmic truth**. In my personal opinion, this shift can be far more dangerous than traditional religions because of two reasons. First, human ego could possibly take a much darker form when **individuals begin to view themselves as "divine" or "chosen ones".** Second, the **spiritual leaders (*Gurus*) take the seat previously occupied by traditional God.**

*Rejecting an external God is relatively easy because God is defined as a separate entity from us which can neither be seen, heard, nor felt. On the other hand, rejecting the idea of an internal spirit feels extremely similar to denying one's own existence and presence in the universe.*

Blind obedience to external authority like God might often indicate lack of rationality and critical thinking in an individual. Nevertheless, ***believing that you have the whole universe within yourself or you are the avatar of the universe itself may pose immeasurably higher intellectual, moral, and social risks.*** Moreover, when someone holding such beliefs does a morally unjustifiable act, the belief itself becomes a justification or excuse. So, there is no external moral, social, and ethical standard left to call it wrong. I personally find no distinction between people believing in such unfalsifiable and egoistic ideas and Dostoyevsky's ***Raskolnikov*** (from [*Crime and Punishment*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_and_Punishment)) in his early days when he firmly believed that he was not an ordinary man and people like him were morally exceptional. Later, ***Raskolnikov*** ends up committing two murders.

When traditional religious institutions lost their grip over the society's power dynamics, *"the old guards"* simply remoulded them into spirituality to retain their influence. Some argue that spirituality as a different practice was into existence long before the decline of religions started. However, my simple question to them is, "Why has it become popular at this phase of our civilization?" "Why wasn't it popular like this back then?" The answer is simple – religions were already powerful so, they did not need spirituality to indoctrinate people and retain their socio-political dominance.

Following the decline of religions, the "old guards" refurbished the concept spirituality and made it fancy. The differences from traditional religions that made spirituality more popular are that **this new replica is more private and self-centred. This could be dangerous. Another difference is that it is far less accountable because questioning yourself is very complex and difficult than questioning God and religion**. Nevertheless, they maintain a **good revenue and authority over public faith**.

While practices like mindfulness, expression of gratitude, and meditation might help to promote self-awareness, connection with nature, and peace of mind, they need not be bought through financial transactions like retreat pricing. I don't understand how people are drawn to services like ***"body-count reset rituals,"*** where the so-called *Gurus* reset the number of sexual partners you've had back to zero. Such services do not directly contribute to promoting self-awareness and mindfulness – they are just a way of looting money from people.

Many people who believe in spirituality practice meditation, gratitude, or a sense of connection to something beyond themselves without any Guru. They neither purchase any product nor believe in any metaphysical claim about being divine. This non-commercial "spiritual" practice is at least better than the previously mentioned practices where people only spend their time, money, and energy unnecessarily.

However, **what people think is spiritual is biological and psychological rather "spiritual"**. The wonderful experiences you have during or after meditating or expressing gratitude is simply a result of your mind releasing neurochemicals like *serotonin, dopamine, oxytocin, etc*. These neurochemicals make you happy and feel at peace – like listening to a music you love. And when people are unaware of this process occurring in their brains – human psychology plays a role to justify or explain it anyhow to us. Consequently, **they relate the neurochemical process to a connection with "the divine"**. If someone believes that God made our nervous system this way, I assume it is better to stay silent and let him/her live in fantasies.

So, practicing meditation and gratitude doesn't need to be contemplated essentially as "spiritual". Following this, I also believe that many institutes teaching yoga, meditation, gratitude, and mindfulness should stop calling themselves anything like "*spiritual wellness/recovery centre*". They can simply promote mental well-being in its naked and scientific form without using "sacred" or "spiritual" packaging. Living with thoughts based on **empirical facts** is **incomparable to** believing in metaphysical ideas and **living by them.**

*Essentially, what spirituality sells us is merely psychological as well as philosophical to some extent. Therefore, the existence of this industry is even more irrelevant and less structured as compared to religions with far more dangers too.*

My genuine recommendation is that if you are someone who feels the need to find and understand your inner-self, visiting a psychiatrist or seeking mental health assistance might help far more than spending time, effort, and money on spiritual rituals. However, if you still insist on walking the spiritual route to validate your "inner self", at least remember this: the "spiritual" claims and geocentric thoughts aren't much different in terms of evidence, logic, and rationality.
